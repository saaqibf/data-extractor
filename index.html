paste a link, drop a PDF, or paste text → get a table → download CSV
How to use this (3 steps)
  1. Get your data in: drop a PDF on the "From a PDF" tab, or copy a table from any website or Excel and paste it in "Paste anything." (The URL tab works on some sites; if a site blocks it, the paste tab always works.)
  2. Check the table that appears below.
  3. Click "Download CSV" and open it in Excel or Google Sheets. Done.
  4. Messy table? Use 🧹 Clean up to trim spaces and drop blank or duplicate rows, click a column heading to sort, or use ▦ Columns to hide what you do not need. You can also type straight into a cell to fix it.
  5. Looking for something specific? Use the 🔎 Find specific things tab to pull out every email, phone number, link, dollar amount or date, or search for any word you want.
Not sure? Click "Show me an example" in the Paste anything tab and watch it work. Nothing you put here leaves your computer.

Paste any web address and press Extract. The tool tries three different ways to read the page, so most sites work. If a page has tables, you get the tables. If it does not, you get the readable text, and it is loaded into the 🔎 Find tab automatically so you can pull out emails, links, amounts or any word you want. If a site blocks every route, copy the part you want and use the Paste anything tab.

Drop one PDF or several at once. The extractor reads the text and rebuilds rows and columns. If the pages share the same columns it also builds a combined table, and if you drop several files it merges them and flags any duplicate rows. Works best on PDFs where the table is real text (most reports), not a scanned photo.

Drop one or more PDFs here, or click to browse

Paste anything with structure: a table copied from a website or Excel, comma separated text, or lines with columns. The extractor figures out the separators.

Looking for something specific instead of a whole table? Paste text (or drop a PDF on the PDF tab first, then come here), pick what you want, and the extractor pulls out every match. Great for grabbing all the contact emails out of a partner list, every dollar amount in a grant report, or every mention of a program name.
